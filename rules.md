# The Quacks of Quedlinburg — Consolidated Rule Sheet

*4+ players, ages 10+, designed by Wolfgang Warsch. Consolidated from the official Schmidt Spiele rulebook.*

---

## ⚠️ Corrections to a claim made earlier in this chat

> "You can always buy more tokens. You never buy white tokens because those are bad."

Both halves of this need correcting, per the rulebook:

1. **You can't always buy more of a given chip.** Every ingredient type has a limited physical supply (e.g. only 20 white 1-chips, 8 white 2-chips, 4 white 3-chips exist in the box, etc., per the Contents list). **Once an ingredient's supply runs out, no one can buy that chip anymore for the rest of the game.**
2. **White chips are not "bad" — they're a core strategic buy.** They're the *only* chips that risk an explosion, but a single higher-value white chip (a 3 or 4) advances your pot several spaces for the "cost" of only one draw and one number added to your explosion total — often a *better* space-per-risk ratio than several small chips. Skilled play regularly includes buying white 3s and 4s.

There's no official name "Boom Berries" in the rulebook — I'll use it below as your house nickname alongside the real terms, but the game's own flavor text calls the explosion-causing white chips **"cherry bombs."**

---

## Theme

Once a year, Quedlinburg holds a 9-day bazaar. Charlatans and quack doctors compete to brew the most potent (and profitable) potion by drawing ingredients from a bag into their cauldron — without letting it boil over.

---

## Components

- 4 pots (player boards, double-sided: standard side + advanced "test-tube" side)
- 1 Scoring Track with Turn Indicator
- 4 flasks
- 4 bags, 4 rat stones, 4 scoring markers, 4 droplets (+4 more for the test-tube variant)
- 1 Flame (turn marker)
- 1 Bonus Die
- 20 rubies
- 215 ingredient chips (+3 replacement chips) across colors: **white, orange, green, blue, red, yellow, purple, black**
- Ingredient books: 2 each of green / blue / red / yellow / purple (grouped into 4 interchangeable **Sets**, marked by 1–4 bookmarks), plus 1 fixed **orange** book and 1 fixed **black** book (with separate pages for 2-player vs. 3–4-player games)
- 24 Fortune Teller cards
- 1 Almanac of Ingredients (full effect reference)

---

## Setup

1. The last player to have cooked something goes first and becomes the start player.
2. Shuffle the Fortune Teller cards into a face-down deck in front of the start player.
3. Place the Scoring Track in the middle of the table. Put the Flame on space 1 of the Turn Indicator. Put the 4 seal tiles on their matching seal spaces, "0" side up.
4. Lay out the **orange** and **black** books — these are used in every game (pick the correct black-book page for your player count).
5. Choose one numbered **Set (1–4)** of green/blue/red/yellow/purple books. For your first game, use **Set 1**. Lay out only the **green, blue, and red** books now — set yellow and purple aside; they enter the game on Turns 2 and 3.
6. Sort chips by color next to their matching books.
7. Each player takes a pot (droplet on the pot's "0" space), a bag, a flask (full side up), a rat stone (on its small trivet), and a scoring marker on their seal tile.
8. Each player fills their bag with the starting chips: **4 white 1-chips, 2 white 2-chips, 1 white 3-chip, 1 orange 1-chip, 1 green 1-chip.**

## The Two Currencies (this trips people up)

| | Coins | Rubies |
|---|---|---|
| **Earned from** | Your scoring space's printed number, each round | Bonus Die, ruby-symbol scoring spaces, some chip effects |
| **Used for** | Buying chips in Evaluation Phase E, **that same round only** | Moving your droplet forward or refilling your flask (End of Turn), and last-round bonus VP |
| **Persists?** | **No** — unspent coins are lost at end of round | **Yes** — rubies carry over between rounds |

---

## Turn Structure (repeats for 9 turns)

### 1. Fortune Teller Card
The player holding the card deck reads the top card aloud, then discards it face-up. Its effect applies to **all players**. Purple cards resolve immediately, before the turn starts; blue cards apply during or at the end of the turn. Ignore any reference to yellow/purple chips or rat-tails if those aren't in play yet.

### 2. The Rats (from Turn 2 onward)
Every player **except the current leader** may move their rat stone forward in their pot before drawing begins. Count the rat-tail symbols on the Scoring Track between your marker and the leader's — that's how many spaces ahead of your droplet you may place your rat stone. Chips drawn this round start filling in after the rat stone instead of the droplet.

### 3. Potions Phase (the push-your-luck draw)
All players draw simultaneously (though not necessarily at the same literal moment):

- Draw one chip from your bag and place it the number of spaces indicated by its value, directly after your last-placed chip (or your droplet/rat stone, if it's your first draw). Empty spaces in between stay empty.
- **Never look inside your bag.**
- After placing a chip, decide: draw again, or stop. Players who've stopped set their bag on the table so others can see they're done.
- If your bag empties, you must stop.

**Explosion rule:** Add up **only the values of white chips** drawn this round. If that total **exceeds 7**, your pot explodes:
- You must stop immediately — no more draws.
- The chip that caused the explosion is still placed.
- Colored-chip effects and Fortune Teller effects still apply normally even after an explosion.
- Non-white chips (orange, green, blue, red, yellow, purple, black) never count toward the explosion total.

**Flask:** If the *last* chip you drew was white, you may flip your flask to return that chip to your bag (undoing it). You cannot do this if that chip's placement caused the explosion. Flip the flask to its "empty" side once used — one use per turn until refilled.

### 4. Evaluation Phase
Your **scoring space** is the space directly after your last placed chip — this holds regardless of whether you exploded.

**A) Bonus Die** — Among players who did *not* explode, whoever reached the highest scoring space rolls the Bonus Die (ties: all tied players roll). Results: 1 VP, 2 VP, 1 ruby, move droplet 1 space forward, or gain 1 orange chip.

**B) Chip Actions** — Starting with the start player, going clockwise, resolve any black, green, or purple chip effects sitting in each pot (per the ingredient books/Almanac — these vary by which Set you're using).

**C) Rubies** — Every player whose scoring space shows a ruby symbol gets 1 ruby, exploded or not.

**D/E) Victory Points *or* Buy Chips** — Non-exploded players get **both** D and E. **Exploded players must choose only one of the two.**
- *D — Victory Points:* Gain the VP printed on your scoring space.
- *E — Buy Chips:* Your scoring space's number = your **coins** for this round. Buy 1 or 2 chips (if 2, they must be different colors) from the market at their books' listed costs. Once a chip type sells out, it's gone for the game. Unspent coins are lost. New purchases go straight into your bag along with your returned drawn chips.

*(Reaching/passing the pot's final space, 33, grants a flat 15 VP and 35 coins, and always qualifies for the Bonus Die roll unless exploded.)*

### 5. End of Turn
Spend **rubies** (not coins), any number of times:
- **2 rubies → move your droplet 1 space forward** (permanent, carries to next round).
- **2 rubies → refill your flask.**

Then: remove rat stones from pots, pass the Fortune Teller deck clockwise (new start player), advance the Flame to the next Turn Indicator space.

### 6. Turn Indicator special events
- **Before Turn 2:** lay out the yellow book — yellow chips become buyable.
- **Before Turn 3:** lay out the purple book — purple chips become buyable.
- **Before Turn 6:** every player adds 1 more white 1-chip to their bag.

---

## Final Round (Turn 9) — special rules
- **"Stir!"** — the start player calls it, and all active players simultaneously reveal a drawn chip (or an empty fist to signal stopping) each round of drawing, rather than drawing privately.
- **No buying chips this round.** Instead of Evaluation Phase E, eligible players may spend, repeatedly: **5 coins → 1 VP**, or **2 rubies → 1 VP.**

---

## End of Game
The player **furthest along the Scoring Track wins.** Ties are broken by whoever filled their pot furthest in the final round. If still tied, all tied players share the title "Biggest Quack of Quedlinburg."

## Optional Variations
- **Simultaneous draws (Turns 1–8):** players may agree to draw at the same time rather than in any order, so no one can react to what others have drawn.
- **Mix your own Sets:** once experienced, combine green/blue/red/yellow/purple books from different numbered Sets instead of using one Set straight through.
- **Test-tube pot side:** flip pots to their alternate side with a second droplet track. Any time you'd move your droplet forward, choose which of your two droplets advances. Moving the test-tube droplet immediately grants a ruby, 1–4 VP, or a set of chips depending on the space reached. Explosion rules still apply as normal even on this side.
